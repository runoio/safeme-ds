## 1. Brand in a nutshell

**What SafeMe is.** A personal-safety app created by a licensed security agency. Trained human Safety Assistants watch over users 24/7. With one tap a user can order an Observation of their route or call for Help. If something goes wrong, the Assistant contacts the user and, when needed, sends emergency services to their exact location.

**Who it is for.**
- Consumers: people walking home after dark, using taxis or ride-hailing, commuting by public transport, running or training outdoors, meeting people from dating apps, coming back from events; parents of children travelling alone; seniors; people with health concerns.
- Businesses (B2B): employers who want an employee benefit, especially for lone fieldwork and client visits (estate agents, service technicians, sales reps, care workers), 2nd/3rd shift returns, commutes and business travel.

**The promise.** A real person is watching over your journey. You are not alone, and your loved ones do not have to stay up waiting.

**The core idea: the safety gap.** Between feeling unsafe and the moment emergency services can act, there is a gap. When you feel uneasy but nothing has happened yet, the emergency number cannot help, because there are no grounds for an intervention. The Safety Assistant fills that gap.

**Brand personality.** Calm, protective, professional, human. The steady expert beside you, not a siren.

**What we avoid.**
- Fear marketing: images of attacks, victims, blood, dark alleys with threatening figures. *Why: SafeMe sells calm and control, not fear.*
- Hype and superlatives without proof ("the safest app in the world"). *Why: a security agency's credibility depends on precise claims.*
- Presenting SafeMe as a replacement for 112/999. *Why: it is not, and saying so is a safety and legal risk.*
- Bots, AI or algorithms as the hero. *Why: the differentiator is a trained human.*
- Playful, jokey or meme tone about danger. *Why: users come to us in stressful moments.*

## 2. Voice and tone

### Principles

- **Calm, not alarmist.** Describe situations plainly and move quickly to what SafeMe does. *Why: anxiety is what the user already has; we bring relief.*
- **Human, not technical.** Lead with the person ("a trained Safety Assistant"), not the technology ("GPS tracking"). *Why: the human is the product.*
- **Concrete, not abstract.** Name real situations: "ktoś idzie za Tobą", "taksówka zjeżdża z trasy", "wracasz do domu późną porą". *Why: specific scenes are believable; abstractions are not.*
- **Short sentences, plain words.** One idea per sentence. *Why: copy is often read on a phone, on the move.*
- **Confident, not boastful.** State what we do, not how great we are. *Why: trust comes from facts, not adjectives.*

### Forms of address

- Address the user directly in the second person: Polish **"Ty"** (informal, capitalized "Ty/Twój/Tobie" in direct address), English **"you"**. *Why: it creates a personal relationship with "your" Assistant.*
- SafeMe speaks as **"my" / "we"** (the team). *Why: there are real people behind the service.*
- In B2B, address the decision-maker as "Ty/you" too, and refer to staff as "Twój zespół / your team", "pracownicy / employees".
- Polish gender-neutral phrasing where natural: "czujesz się zagrożony lub zagrożona".

### Capitalization and naming

- **Obserwacja** (Observation) and **Pomoc** (Help) are proper nouns, always capitalized, in every inflection: "Zamów Obserwację", "wezwij Pomoc", "Nielimitowane Obserwacje tras". *Why: they are the two named product actions.*
- **Asystent Bezpieczeństwa** / **Safety Assistant** is always capitalized. Short form "Asystent" / "Assistant" is fine after the first mention.
- **SafeMe** is written as one word, capital S and M. Never "Safe Me", "SAFEME", "Safeme".
- **safety gap** stays in English in Polish copy, lowercase, introduced as "tzw. safety gap" the first time.
- Sentence case for headings and buttons. Uppercase only for short eyebrows/labels (1–4 words).

### Vocabulary

Use:
- PL: Asystent Bezpieczeństwa, czuwa, reaguje, jednym kliknięciem / jednym dotknięciem, 24/7, prawdziwa osoba, człowiek, nie bot, koncesjonowana agencja ochrony, poczuj się bezpiecznie, testuj 7 dni za darmo, Twoi bliscy, spokój.
- EN: Safety Assistant, watches over, responds, one tap, 24/7, a real person, a person not a bot, licensed security agency, feel safe, try it free for 7 days, your loved ones.

Avoid:
- "ofiara / victim", "atak / attack" as headline words. *Why: frames the user as helpless.*
- "śledzenie / tracking", "inwigilacja / surveillance". Use "Obserwacja trasy / Observation of your route". *Why: tracking suggests control over the user, not care for them.*
- "AI", "algorytm", "bot", "chatbot" as a description of the service. *Why: it is a human service.*
- "gwarantujemy bezpieczeństwo / we guarantee your safety", "100% bezpieczny". *Why: no one can promise that; it is a false claim.*
- "zamiast 112 / instead of 999". *Why: SafeMe works before and alongside emergency services.*
- "panika", "horror", "koszmar", exclamation marks in a row, emoji outside social media.
- "najlepsza / najbezpieczniejsza / jedyna" without a source.

### Good and bad examples

1. Hero headline
   - Good (PL): „Prawdziwa osoba czuwa nad Twoją drogą do domu.”
   - Good (EN): "A real person watching your journey home."
   - Bad: „Nie daj się zaatakować w drodze do domu!” *Why bad: fear framing and exclamation.*

2. Explaining the service
   - Good (PL): „Powiedz aplikacji, dokąd idziesz. Przeszkolony Asystent Bezpieczeństwa na żywo śledzi Twoją trasę, we dnie i w nocy.”
   - Good (EN): "Tell the app where you're going. A trained Safety Assistant follows your route live, day and night."
   - Bad: „Nasz zaawansowany algorytm AI monitoruje Twoją lokalizację GPS.” *Why bad: technology and AI as the hero.*

3. Relation to emergency services
   - Good (PL): „SafeMe działa wcześniej – gdy czujesz się niepewnie, ale nie ma jeszcze podstaw do zgłoszenia.”
   - Good (EN): "SafeMe is for the moments before that, when you feel unsafe but there's nothing to report yet."
   - Bad: „Zapomnij o 112 – masz SafeMe.” *Why bad: dangerous and false.*

4. Call to action
   - Good (PL): „Poczuj się bezpiecznie. Pobierz aplikację i testuj przez 7 dni za darmo.”
   - Good (EN): "Feel safe. Download the app and try it free for 7 days."
   - Bad: „KUP TERAZ!!! Promocja tylko dziś!” *Why bad: shouting, invented urgency.*

5. Loved ones
   - Good (PL): „To my reagujemy, a nie rodzina czy znajomi, którzy nie wiedzą, jak zareagować.”
   - Good (EN): "It is us who react, not family or friends who may not know what to do."
   - Bad: „Twoja rodzina nie musi się już o Ciebie martwić, bo nic Ci nie grozi.” *Why bad: promises an outcome we cannot guarantee.*

6. Naming the actions
   - Good (PL): „Jednym kliknięciem zamówisz Obserwację trasy lub wezwiesz Pomoc.”
   - Good (EN): "With one tap you can order an Observation of your route or call for Help."
   - Bad: „Kliknij, żeby obserwować trasę albo poprosić o pomoc.” *Why bad: actions not named as proper nouns.*

7. B2B
   - Good (PL): „Bezpieczeństwo jako benefit dla Twojego zespołu.”
   - Good (EN): "Safety as a benefit for your team."
   - Bad: „Zmniejsz odpowiedzialność prawną firmy za incydenty pracowników.” *Why bad: legal claim we have not approved, and a cold, defensive frame.*

8. Social caption
   - Good (PL): „Taksówka zjechała z trasy? Asystent Bezpieczeństwa widzi to od razu i dzwoni do Ciebie.”
   - Bad: „Ta historia zmrozi Ci krew w żyłach…” *Why bad: clickbait fear.*

## 3. Visual identity

### Colors

Four brand colors anchor everything. Each has a strict job.

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Brand red | #FF1616 | Logo mark (sygnet) and ONE accent fragment in a headline. Nothing else. |
| Action red (SOS) | #ED3745 | Help/SOS/alarm only: the Help button, video play buttons, the 112/999 point in the safety-gap diagram, danger steps. |
| Dark blue (navy) | #0E1353 | Trust color. Headings and strong text, dark backgrounds, default primary buttons. |
| Action blue | #5BADFF | Safe/informational states: Observation, rings and halos, check icons, eyebrows and text on navy. |
| Accessible blue | #006DDB | Links and filled blue buttons with white text (the highlighted option in a set). |

Rules:
- **Action red #ED3745 means "help".** Never use it for decoration, sale badges, prices or highlights. *Why: its scarcity is the signal; overuse would make the emergency action invisible.*
- **Brand red #FF1616 appears at most once per screen in text** (one headline fragment) plus the logo. *Why: one red accent focuses attention; several make a page look alarming.*
- **Never put white text on #5BADFF.** Use #006DDB for blue buttons with white text. *Why: white on #5BADFF fails contrast (2.4:1); #006DDB passes (4.9:1).*
- **Navy is the default background for impact; light grey is the default for reading.** *Why: these two contexts are the whole brand system.*
- Do not invent new brand colors. Use the neutrals below for everything else.

Neutrals (cool grey, tuned towards navy):
- #FFFFFF white (cards), #F6F7FB page background, #ECEEF5 sunken areas, #DDE0EC subtle borders, #C3C7DA default borders, #9AA0BC strong borders, #6E7492 muted text, #333853 body text, #0E1353 strong text.
- Never use warm greys or pure black text. *Why: warm grey clashes with the cool navy palette.*

Supporting:
- Deeper navy #080B36 and indigo #1B2170 / #232A82 for gradients.
- Status green #1F9D57 (success, "available" dot), warning amber #E8A317. Use only for real states, never decoration.

Gradients (only these, only on dark sections):
- Hero: diagonal from #232A82 through #0E1353 to #080B22.
- Deep: vertical from #1B2170 through #0E1353 to black.
- Spotlight: radial glow #2A3299 at the center fading to #0E1353 and #05060F, used behind a phone.
- *Why: gradients stay inside the navy family so they read as depth, not decoration.* No rainbow, purple-pink or multi-hue gradients.

### Typography

- **Headings: Montserrat**, weights 600–800. Hero headlines extrabold (800), tight letter spacing (about -2%), line height about 1.1. Section headings bold (700). *Why: Montserrat's geometric shapes read as solid and infrastructural.*
- **Body: Inter**, weights 400–600, line height 1.5–1.65. *Why: Inter stays legible at small sizes and on screens.*
- Fallback if fonts are unavailable: system sans-serif (Segoe UI, San Francisco, Helvetica). Never a serif, script or display font.
- Web scale: hero 56 px, H1 40, H2 32, H3 24, H4 20, large body 18, body 16, small 14, caption 12 (minimum).
- Uppercase eyebrows: Montserrat bold, 12–13 px, letter spacing 8%, max 4 words.
- One headline accent: a single fragment of the headline in brand red #FF1616, carrying the value ("Prawdziwa osoba", "real person", "SafeMe", "360 dni bezpieczeństwa"). Never two accents, never the whole headline.

### Logo

Versions:
- **Full logo (wordmark + sygnet)**: navy/black version for light backgrounds, white version for dark backgrounds.
- **Sygnet**: three red chevrons forming an "E". Used as app icon, avatar, favicon, and small brand mark.
- **Sub-brand "SafeMe Nieruchomości"**: the full logo with the word "Nieruchomości" set in Montserrat 600 below, scaled to the wordmark width. Use only for the real-estate offer.

Rules:
- The sygnet is always brand red #FF1616, except in single-color (all-black or all-white) print. *Why: the red chevrons are the most recognizable part of the brand.*
- **Clear space**: keep empty space around the logo equal to half the height of the sygnet on all sides. *Why: crowded logos lose authority.*
- **Minimum size**: full logo 80 px wide on screen / 20 mm in print; sygnet 16 px / 4 mm. *Why: below this the chevrons blur together.*
- Place the logo on solid navy, white or light grey. On photos, only over a calm, dark or light area with enough contrast.

Do / Don't:
- Do use the white logo on navy and the navy/black logo on white.
- Do keep the original proportions.
- Don't recolor the wordmark (no blue, no gradient, no red wordmark).
- Don't stretch, rotate, outline, add shadows or effects.
- Don't recreate the logo in another font or draw your own chevrons.
- Don't place the logo inside a red shape or next to another red element.
- Don't write "SafeMe" in text as a substitute for the logo on covers.

### Icons

- Line icons, 2 px stroke, rounded joins and ends (Lucide is the official icon set). Sizes 18–24 px in UI. *Why: a thin, even line matches the calm, professional tone.* Don't mix Lucide with other icon sets.
- Colors: navy for interactive, muted grey for decorative, action blue #5BADFF for safe/check, action red only for Help.
- Typical icons: map pin, navigation, phone, message, shield with check, user, car, bus.
- The two core actions have fixed icons: **Pomoc / Help = a headset (Lucide "headset")**, **Obserwacja / Observation = a navigation arrow (Lucide "navigation")**. *Why: the headset says a real person answers, and it stays legible at small sizes.* Never use a bell, siren or exclamation mark for Help.
- Don't use filled, 3D, gradient or emoji-style icons. Don't use emoji in product UI, web copy or as bullet points (social media is the only exception, see 4.4).

### Photography and imagery

- **Mood**: calm, cool-toned, everyday. Real people in real situations (walking at dusk, in a taxi, on a platform, running in the park), looking composed, often with a phone. *Why: we show the moment of feeling safe, not the threat.*
- **Light**: evening and night scenes are on-brand when they feel peaceful (street lights, warm windows), not menacing.
- **People**: diverse in age (teens, adults, seniors), gender and situation. For B2B: professionals at work (estate agents, technicians, care workers).
- **Phone mockups**: show the real app screen. On dark backgrounds, add a soft blue glow behind the phone (action blue, slow breathing pulse, never red).
- Don't: staged attacks, threatening silhouettes, hooded figures, crying victims, police tape, blood, red-alert filters, heavy stock clichés (hands on shield, padlock icons on a globe).
- If you cannot provide a real photo, leave a clearly labeled placeholder. Do not invent AI images of SafeMe staff or of the app UI.

### Shape, radius, shadow, spacing

- **Corner radius**: cards 16 px; large panels, sections and video frames 24 px; inputs 10 px; buttons and chips fully rounded (pill). *Why: soft but not childish; the pill button is a brand signature.*
- **Shadows**: soft, low-opacity and tinted navy (never grey-black). Small: 0 2 px 6 px at 8% navy; large: 0 16 px 40 px at 14% navy. *Why: tinted shadows keep surfaces feeling light and cool.*
- The Help button has a red glow; Observation has a blue glow. No other glows.
- **Cards**: white surface, 1 px light cool-grey border plus a soft shadow. Never a colored left border stripe.
- **Spacing**: 4 px grid. Common steps 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80 px. Generous white space; sections on web are separated by about 80 px.
- **Motion**: quick and purposeful (120–320 ms). Looping animations are slow (4 s or more) and blue. *Why: fast or red pulses read as alarm.*

## 4. Guidelines per format

### 4.1 Web and landing pages

Structure:
- Page width max 1120 px, mobile-first.
- **Hero** (dark navy gradient): eyebrow → headline with one brand-red accent → subtitle (max ~2 lines on desktop) → CTA (App Store and Google Play badges, or one button) with the italic note "Testuj 7 dni za darmo." / "Try it free for 7 days." in the same row → trust line of exactly 3 short claims with blue check icons → phone mockup with blue glow.
- Recommended order for a consumer page: Hero → How it works (3 steps + vertical video) → Who is your Safety Assistant → Call for help (dark panel, red Help button) → Packages → Reviews → Employee benefit teaser → Safety gap diagram → Closing CTA.
- B2B page: Hero with "Umów spotkanie / Book a meeting" → What SafeMe for business is → How it works → When employees use it → What the company gains → Onboarding steps → Cafeteria platforms → Pricing tiers → Awards → Reviews → FAQ → Contact person CTA.
- Landing pages (segments: Zdrowie/Health, Nastolatki/Teens, Seniorzy/Seniors, Randki/Dating, Praca/Commute, Taxi, Prezent/Gift) have no navigation and one goal: download.

Rules:
- One primary CTA per section. *Why: competing CTAs reduce action.*
- Buttons: navy by default; #006DDB for the single highlighted option (e.g. the "Family" plan); white button on dark panels; action red only for Help.
- Max 2 dark panels per page, plus the hero. *Why: rhythm between light and dark keeps long pages readable.*
- Two section-heading sizes only: one for every section heading, a larger one for the closing CTA.
- The safety-gap diagram reads left to right: "Czujesz niepokój / Feeling unsafe" → "safety gap · SafeMe" (blue) → "112" (PL) or "999" (UK) as a red point.
- Trust claims must be literally true and page-specific.

### 4.2 Presentations

Background, text-density and video rules below are confirmed. Narrative structure, logo placement and chart rules are **PROPOSAL – to be confirmed**.

- Format 16:9. Two backgrounds only: navy #0E1353 (cover, section dividers, closing) and light #F6F7FB or white (content). *Why: two backgrounds create rhythm without noise.*
- Narrative structure (10–15 slides): 1) cover with promise, 2) the problem (situations of feeling unsafe), 3) the safety gap, 4) how SafeMe works (Observation and Help), 5) the Safety Assistant (human, licensed agency), 6) use cases, 7) proof (awards, reviews), 8) offer and prices, 9) next step / contact.
- One message per slide. The slide title is a full sentence stating that message. *Why: the audience should get the point from titles alone.*
- Text: max 50 words per content slide, max 4 bullets. *Why: decks are often sent and read without a presenter, so slides need enough text to stand alone.* Minimum 24 pt body, titles 40–60 pt Montserrat bold/extrabold.
- Logo: full logo on the cover and closing slide; small sygnet in a corner of content slides. Not on every slide in large size.
- Colors in charts: navy for SafeMe data, action blue for a second series, cool greys for comparisons. Never red for data unless it represents an emergency. No 3D charts, no pie charts with more than 4 slices.
- Images: full-bleed calm photos on section dividers; phone mockups on product slides.
- Video in decks: embed in a vertical 9:16 frame next to text, never stretched to fill a 16:9 slide.

### 4.3 Video

**Videos are ALWAYS vertical 9:16 (e.g. 1080 × 1920). No exceptions: no horizontal versions, no cropping to landscape, no letterboxing a vertical video into a horizontal frame.** *Why: SafeMe content lives on phones and short-form platforms, and all existing videos are vertical.* When a vertical video is placed on a web page or slide, keep it 9:16 in a frame with 24 px rounded corners and a red (#ED3745) circular play button.

Confirmed rules:
- Length: up to 60 s for every video. Aim for the shortest length that tells the story. *Why: 60 s fits Reels, TikTok and Shorts and leaves room for a full situation and resolution.*
- Structure: hook in the first 2 s (a recognizable situation: a taxi turning off the route, footsteps behind you) → SafeMe action (tap, Assistant responds) → calm resolution → CTA.
- Captions: always burned in, animated word by word (short-form style). Montserrat bold, white text with the currently spoken word highlighted in action blue #5BADFF, on a navy #0E1353 semi-opaque rounded box. Max 2 lines, max ~32 characters per line, inside the safe zone (not in the top 15% or bottom 20% of the frame where platform UI sits). Never highlight words in red. *Why: most viewers watch muted, and word-by-word captions hold attention; blue keeps the highlight calm.*
- No separate outro screen. The CTA appears as captions in the last shot of the story ("Testuj 7 dni za darmo" / "Try it free for 7 days"). *Why: a cut to a branded end card loses viewers before the CTA.*
- Logo: no logo during the video. The white full logo appears only in the last shot, together with the CTA captions. *Why: an early logo reads as an ad and lowers watch time.*
- Music: calm, low-tempo ambient or soft electronic. No horror stings, sirens or alarm sounds as music. *Why: the music should lower tension, not build it.*
- Colors: navy and cool tones in grading; red only on the Help button or the 112/999 point.

**PROPOSAL – to be confirmed**:
- Pace: calm. 1 idea per shot, shots 1.5–3 s. No flashing, no strobe, no shaky "horror" camera.
- No logo intro.
- Voice-over: calm, warm, confident, at normal speaking pace.
- Tone: reassuring. The threat is implied, never shown violently.

### 4.4 Social media

Formats, emoji and hashtag rules are confirmed. Layout and copy structure are **PROPOSAL – to be confirmed**.

Formats:
- Reels / TikTok / Shorts: 9:16 vertical (1080 × 1920), see Video rules.
- Feed post: 4:5 (1080 × 1350) and 1:1 (1080 × 1080) are equally valid; pick by platform and content.
- Stories: 9:16.
- LinkedIn (B2B): 1:1 or 4:5 image, or document carousel.

Layout:
- Navy or light background, one headline (max ~8 words) in Montserrat extrabold, optional one brand-red accent word, small logo or sygnet in a corner.
- Carousels: slide 1 = hook, slides 2–5 = one point each, last slide = CTA with logo.
- Leave the outer 60 px free of text.

Copy:
- First line is the hook: a concrete situation or question ("Taksówka zjechała z trasy?").
- 2–4 short sentences, then one CTA ("Testuj 7 dni za darmo – link w bio." / "Try it free for 7 days – link in bio.").
- Emoji: allowed in moderation, 1–3 per post, never in the headline on the graphic and never as alarm symbols (🚨, ⚠️, 🆘). *Why: a light touch keeps posts native to the platform without undermining the calm tone.*
- Reviews: only real testimonials, quoted verbatim, with first name. Never write new testimonials.

Hashtags: 0–2 per post, chosen from the lists below; #SafeMe first if used. *Why: few hashtags look professional and keep the caption readable.*
- PL: #SafeMe #AsystentBezpieczeństwa #bezpieczeństwo #bezpiecznypowrót #bezpiecznawdrodze
- UK: #SafeMe #SafetyAssistant #PersonalSafety #GetHomeSafe #SafeJourney
- B2B (LinkedIn): #EmployeeBenefits #BenefitPracowniczy #LoneWorker #DutyOfCare

Handles: Instagram / TikTok PL @safeme.official, UK @safeme.uk. Facebook, LinkedIn (company page "safemeapp") and YouTube are shared.

### 4.5 Print and B2B materials

**PROPOSAL – to be confirmed** unless noted.

- Formats: A4 one-pagers and offers, A5/DL leaflets, roll-ups (85 × 200 cm), voucher cards.
- Layout: navy header or cover block, white body, generous margins (at least 15 mm). Logo top-left or centered on the cover.
- Body text minimum 9 pt (leaflets) / 10–11 pt (documents); headings Montserrat bold.
- Colors: build navy and red in CMYK from the hex values and proof before printing. *Why: #FF1616 and #ED3745 shift strongly in CMYK.*
- Every B2B piece ends with a contact block: name, role and email, plus the demo-booking link or a QR code.
- B2B prices are per user per month, net of the retail price, and must match Section 5 exactly.
- Always include company legal details on offers and contracts (Section 5).

## 5. Facts you must not change

**Never invent prices, numbers, statistics, promises, coverage, testimonials, awards or partners.** If a fact you need is not below, ask the user. *Why: SafeMe is a licensed security service; a wrong claim is a legal and trust problem.*

### The two core actions
- **Obserwacja / Observation**: the user sets destination and mode of transport; the Safety Assistant monitors the route and reacts if anything deviates from plan.
- **Pomoc / Help**: one tap in a threatening situation; the Assistant attempts phone contact, assesses the situation, and passes the report with location to the appropriate services if needed.
- Both are always capitalized and **unlimited in every plan**.

### Coverage
- 24/7 in **Poland and the United Kingdom**. Never claim other countries.
- Emergency number by market: **PL = 112, UK = 999**. Use the right one in every piece.
- SafeMe does not replace 112/999. If someone is in immediate danger, they should call 112/999.

### Proof points
